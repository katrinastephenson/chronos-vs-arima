01
The question

Every ARIMA forecast needs an analyst. A pretrained model claims it does not.

ARIMA, for each new series

  1. Difference the series until it is stationary.
  2. Read the ACF and PACF and choose the orders (p, d, q).
  3. Estimate the coefficients.
  4. Check the residuals, and revise if they fail.

A pretrained forecasting model

  1. Pass in the history. Get a forecast back.

Pretrained models remove the need to train a model from scratch for each forecasting problem.⁠[1]

Does skipping the work cost accuracy? We tested it on two series a bank forecasts routinely: the 30-year mortgage rate and commercial bank deposits.

03
In this corner · ARIMA

ARIMA: one model for each series, identified by hand

  1. Difference the series until it looks stationary.
  2. Read the ACF and PACF of the differences to choose p and q.⁠[2,7]
  3. Compare candidate orders by BIC and estimate by maximum likelihood.
  4. Check the residuals: ACF and the Ljung-Box test.⁠[8]
  5. Keep the orders fixed and re-estimate the coefficients at each of the 8 forecast origins.
The model: ARIMA(p, 1, q) with drift

ϕ(B) (∇xt − β) = θ(B) wt

  • ∇xt = xt − xt−1: the week-to-week change.
  • ϕ(B), θ(B): the autoregressive and moving-average polynomials in the backshift operator B, of orders p and q.
  • β: the drift, the mean weekly change. Zero for a model without drift.
  • wt: white noise.

80% interval: the forecast plus or minus 1.28 standard errors.⁠[2]

In the other corner · Chronos-2

Chronos-2: a pretrained model that forecasts without being fitted to our data

  • What it is. A time series forecasting model from Amazon Web Services, released in 2025.⁠[1]
  • Pretrained. Its parameters were learned in advance, from a large collection of other time series, both real and synthetic.⁠[1]
  • Zero-shot. It forecasts a series it has never seen, with no additional training. We pass in the history and get a forecast back.⁠[1]
  • Same family as language models. Its network is a transformer encoder that follows the design of the T5 language model.⁠[1]

06
The rules

Eight rounds of 13 weeks, and no model sees past its origin

  • Same history for both models: January 2000 up to the origin, and nothing after it.
  • Each round: forecast the next 13 weeks. Rounds do not overlap.
  • Then the truth is revealed: the 13 actual values join the history for the next round.
  • 8 × 13 = 104 scored weeks for each series.
  • The benchmark: the naive forecast, the last observed value carried forward.
  • Scoring: mean absolute error (MAE), MAE relative to naive, and 80% interval coverage. The lower MAE wins the round.

11
Final verdict

Final score: one match each, and the trend decided it

  1. No trend, no gap. On the mortgage rate, zero-shot Chronos-2 matched a hand-built ARIMA. Neither beat the naive forecast by more than 5%.
  2. The trend decided it. On deposits, ARIMA's drift term projected the growth and cut the error in half.
  3. Two kinds of work. ARIMA takes effort up front and can be checked before it forecasts. Chronos-2 takes none, and can only be judged afterward.

Limitations

  • Two series, one horizon (13 weeks), eight windows.
  • Chronos-2's deposit forecasts changed with the length of history supplied: in a follow-up check, MAE relative to naive fell from 1.01 to 0.42 with five years of history.
  • The ARIMA residuals show some remaining autocorrelation.
